A. Executive summary

Northbridge declared four AI systems. Two are likely in scope for Article 50, one is likely out of scope, and one cannot be resolved without clarifying who the provider is.

The most significant gap is not a missing obligation — it is timing. The support assistant does disclose that it is an AI, but the disclosure sits in the chat widget’s collapsed help panel, which a user can reach the assistant without opening. Article 50(5) requires the information at the latest at the time of the first interaction (CL-06).

The second item worth attention is the marketing image generator. It has no machine-readable marking. Because the system was placed on the market before 2 August 2026, the marking obligation in Article 50(2) is subject to the transitional period ending 2 December 2026 (CL-07) — roughly three months from this assessment.

Do first:

  1. Move the AI disclosure in the support assistant to first contact (S-01).
  2. Decide the machine-readable marking approach for generated images before 2 December 2026 (S-02).
  3. Resolve the provider/deployer question on the embedded vendor chatbot (S-04) — until that is settled, its obligations cannot be assigned.

D. Transparency gap analysis — S-01

QuestionCurrent stateAssessment
Is there a disclosure?Yes — “This assistant uses AI.”Wording is adequate and plain.
Where does it appear?Inside the collapsed “Help & policies” panel of the widget.Gap. Reachable, but not on the path a user actually takes.
When does the user see it?Only if the panel is opened.Gap. 50(5) requires it at the latest at first interaction (CL-06).
Is it clear and distinguishable?Yes, once found.Placement is the issue, not the wording.

The fix here is not writing new copy. It is moving copy that already exists to a place the user passes through.

Purpose is narrow: showing what was implemented and when. Enforcement sits with national market surveillance authorities (CL-10).

H. Legal review flags

LEGAL_REVIEW_RECOMMENDED

  1. S-01 / 50(2) scope. Whether conversational replies to a single user count as synthetic content requiring machine-readable marking. Turns on scope, not on facts we can gather from you.
  2. S-02 / “resembles real”. Whether the stylised images engage the deepfake disclosure duty, and whether the creative carve-out applies (CL-08).
  3. S-04 / role. Provider or deployer. A contractual question, not a technical one — and it decides which paragraph applies.

These are listed, not resolved. Resolving them is legal work, and this assessment does not do legal work.
